One serif or two?

The question behind the dual-serif setup: Fraunces reads as too loosely fitted at small sizes, and the fix was to introduce a second serif for running text. But loose fit is a typographic problem, and typographic problems are often fixable with tracking, optical sizing, and variable axes rather than a whole extra font. Same note text, five ways.

Theme
Real note text, at the sizes it actually appears in the app.

Fraunces is a variable font with four axes: wght, opsz (9–144), SOFT (0–100) and WONK (0–1). The wonky terminals and the wide default fit are the same features that make it good at 32px and bad at 14px. Candidate 2 switches WONK off, flattens SOFT, and pulls tracking in by 0.012em — three lines of CSS, no second font file.

The honest summary: tracking and axis changes fix roughly most of what you objected to, but not all of it. Fraunces tuned at 15px reads noticeably better than Fraunces raw — the spacing problem you identified is genuinely fixable. What doesn't go away is that a display face with high optical contrast is doing a job it wasn't drawn for once it has to carry four-line notes you'll actually reread. If the notes stay the centre of the app, the text face deserves to be chosen for that job specifically, and "one family everywhere" is only free if you're willing to spend some of that comfort.
