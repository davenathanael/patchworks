Candidate 1 — Cards

Round 1's B, pulled tighter. The whitespace is roughly halved, corner radius drops from 14px to 5px, and the sans-only typography is replaced by Fraunces for titles and JetBrains Mono for metadata. Tags are plain #hashtags rather than pills. Navigation lives in a top strip on desktop and bottom tabs on mobile — deliberately not a rail, because a rail is the thing that made round 1's B feel like a desktop app shrunk down. Items are cards on mobile and collapse into an aligned row past 44rem; the data is identical, only the arrangement changes.

Verdict on this direction

Strongest at — notes, reading, and mobile. The note is a first-class part of the item here rather than a sub-line or an inspector field, which is the thing round 1's ledger could not do. The mobile composer → full-screen sheet is the most natural capture gesture of the three. Typographically it's the calmest and least generated-looking.

Still weak at — cross-item comparison, which is inherent to the card model and not fixable within this IA. If organize-heavy use ever becomes the dominant activity rather than one of three equal ones, this is the candidate that suffers. It also has the least room to grow into bulk operations, and the top-strip navigation on desktop is a weaker pattern than a rail for a user with nine collections.

The one decision this candidate forces: whether feed items are ephemeral (path A) or auto-bookmarked (path B). This IA is fairly neutral on it — both work — so it can be decided later on its own merits. That's a point in this direction's favour.
