Candidate D — Split Deck

The interaction-model divergence. Three panes, all visible: folders on the left, a stream in the middle, and the thing itself on the right. The structural bet is that bookmarks and feed items are the same kind of object — something found on the internet worth keeping — so they live in one mixed stream and share one action vocabulary. Triaging a feed item and filing a bookmark are the same three keystrokes. A command palette covers everything else. On mobile the panes collapse to list → item, the standard mail-client degradation.

Note the deliberate palette break: this is a cool neutral with a single blue accent, not the current warm mocha. Part of the question here is whether "calm" is a property of the palette at all, or only of the layout.

1 · The deck

Everything at once: where you are, what's there, and what it says. Selecting an item loads it in the reader pane without a page transition, so a scan down 20 items costs one page load rather than 20. The footer is the triage surface — the same three buttons appear whether the item came from a feed or from your own typing.

What's new in Go 1.26: iterators everywhere

Range-over-func finally landed in the standard library. Here's what changes for iterators, errors, and allocation — and why the compiler team decided the old two-value range was the wrong shape.

The iterator proposal has been in the works for three release cycles, and the shape that finally shipped is not the one most people expected.

Instead of seq[V] with a separate yield function, Go 1.26 adopts range-over-func directly. A function of the shape func(yield func(V) bool) is now rangeable, which means the iterator and the loop are the same abstraction.

The consequence is that iteration can no longer be resumed after the loop exits — and the committee decided that was the right trade.

For library authors this mostly simplifies things. The three-method iterator interface goes away, replaced by one method with a callback. Allocation profile improves in the common case because the yield closure can be stack-allocated.

Postgres VACUUM, finally explained properly

Vacuum is the most misunderstood routine in PostgreSQL, and most of the confusion comes from documentation that describes the mechanism accurately but the purpose poorly.

The purpose is simple: reclaim space and keep the visibility map honest. Everything downstream — index bloat, autovacuum thresholds, the aggressive-worker heuristics — is a consequence of those two jobs being expensive.

Vacuum exists to answer two questions: where is the dead space, and who is allowed to see which row version.

The multixact section is the one I need for the pagination work. Everything before it is a rehash of the official docs.

What this direction is good and bad for

Good for — the RSS feature, by a wide margin. This is the only candidate where "add a feed" and "save a bookmark" are the same gesture in the same list, which is the whole point of having both. It also handles bulk operations naturally (⌘A then an action on the reader pane), and the constant-position layout means you never lose your place while triaging. The reader pane makes scanning twenty items one interaction rather than twenty page loads, which is the retrieve-heavy win.

Bad for — everything else, a bit. Three panes means less room for the list on any screen, and the mobile degradation is genuinely worse than B's (which was designed mobile-first) and only marginally better than C's. Selection-dependent actions are a real accessibility and discoverability cost — nothing tells a first-time visitor that s saves. Quick-add as a modal fights the layout rather than fitting it. And the cool palette is a gamble: it may read as more neutral and modern, or as a departure from a warm identity you'd rather keep.

The open question if you pick this direction: does merging feeds and bookmarks into one stream feel clarifying or like a loss of structure? It's a real fork in the road for the feature set, independent of layout — and it's the only direction that forces you to actually decide it.
